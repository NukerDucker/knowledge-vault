# How to Write a Workplace Report

By Arjarn Krongthip Junyaem

## What Is a Report?

A report is a structured way of presenting **information, facts, results, problems, findings, or recommendations** so the reader can understand the situation and make decisions.

A good workplace report answers four basic questions:
- What happened?
- What did you do?
- What was the result?
- What is the next step?

## Academic Report vs. Workplace Report

| Academic Report | Workplace Report |
|---|---|
| Focuses on theories and references | Focuses on facts and decisions |
| Often contains more detail | Usually concise and direct |
| Explains concepts | Focuses on results |
| May include a literature review | May not include a literature review |
| Written mainly for lecturers | Written for supervisors, managers, or clients |
| May be many pages long | May be only 1–3 pages |

> **Key principle: Write for the reader, not for yourself. Always ask: "What does the reader need to know?"**

## Why Is Report Writing Important?

Types of reports you may write: Internship, Daily, Weekly, Progress, Meeting, Project, Problem, Incident, Survey, Performance.

Good report writing helps to:
- Communicate information clearly
- Show responsibility and professionalism
- Help supervisors monitor progress
- Support problem-solving and decision-making
- Provide a record of work
- Present results in an organized way

## The 5Cs of Good Report Writing

1. **Clear** — Use simple and direct language
   - Poor: "The project had some problems and many things happened."
   - Better: "The project was delayed for two days because the supplier delivered the materials late."

2. **Concise** — Avoid unnecessary words
   - Poor: "Due to the fact that the meeting started later than the time that had originally been planned..."
   - Better: "The meeting started 20 minutes late."

3. **Correct** — Check all details: facts, numbers, dates, names, grammar, spelling. Incorrect information reduces credibility.

4. **Complete** — Include all important information. Use **5W1H**: Who? What? When? Where? Why? How?

5. **Consistent** — Use the same format throughout: font, headings, date format, bullet points, table style, terminology.

## Suggested Report Format (6 sections)

1. **Title** — Write a clear and specific title
2. **Introduction / Purpose** — Explain why the report is being written
3. **Findings** — Present important facts, information, or results
4. **Problems / Causes** — Explain main problems, possible causes, and effects
5. **Recommendations** — Suggest 2–3 practical solutions or actions
6. **Conclusion** — Summarize the main findings and recommendations

**Writing formula:** Fact → Problem → Cause → Recommendation → Conclusion

## Assignment Cases (choose one)

### Case 1: Customer Complaints Report
ABC Café received 18 complaints in August (normally ~5/month).
- Slow service: 8 / Incorrect food orders: 5 / Cleanliness: 3 / Impolite staff: 2
- Task: Write a report on the increase and recommend solutions.
- Suggested title: *Report on Customer Complaints in August*

### Case 2: Late Attendance Report
HR found 12/30 Production Dept employees arrived late (35 cases total).
- Reasons: traffic, long commute, oversleeping, shuttle bus issues
- Effect: production line started 10–15 min late
- Task: Write a report on employee lateness, causes, effects, and recommendations.
- Suggested title: *Report on Employee Late Attendance*

### Case 3: Workplace Accident Report
Warehouse employee tripped over a box at 2:30 pm, suffered minor ankle injury.
- Investigation: boxes in walkway, no warning sign, walkway not checked
- Task: Write an incident report — what happened, causes, actions taken, preventive measures.
- Suggested title: *Workplace Accident Report*

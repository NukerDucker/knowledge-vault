## Analysis of Data

### Risks that move the stock price

1. **Health / consumer trends.** Shift away from sugary drinks; sugar taxes and regulation in many countries pressure soda volume. Core long-term risk for a soft-drink company.
2. **Currency (FX) risk.** ~60%+ of revenue is outside the US. A strong US dollar shrinks reported overseas earnings even when local sales are fine.
3. **Input cost / inflation.** Prices of sugar, aluminum (cans), and packaging swing margins. Inflation raises costs faster than they can raise prices.
4. **Competition.** PepsiCo, private-label store brands, and healthier beverage startups compete on price and shelf space.
5. **Regulatory / litigation.** Sugar taxes, labeling rules, plastic/environmental regulation, and marketing restrictions add cost and limit reach.
6. **Slow-growth / interest-rate sensitivity.** Mature business with modest growth; treated like a bond-proxy dividend stock, so rising interest rates can pull the price down.

### Is it a good investment?

Solid defensive / income investment — low volatility.

**For:** Global brand moat, wide distribution, steady cash flow, long history of dividend growth (Dividend King). Low beta — small monthly swings (see table: mostly under ±$5). Holds up in recessions because people keep buying cheap drinks.

**Against:** Slow growth, health headwinds, FX drag. Won't produce big capital gains like a tech stock.

**Verdict:** Good for a conservative investor wanting stable value + reliable dividends rather than fast growth. Low risk, low volatility — the opposite profile of NVIDIA. Suitable as a defensive core holding; not the pick if the goal is aggressive capital appreciation.

### Summary (worksheet)

Coca-Cola (KO) fluctuated in a stable range of $76-$89 between March and July 2026, approaching its 52-week high price of $90.92. The monthly price fluctuation remained limited (within ±$5). Primary risks: evolving health trends, which would decrease the demand for beverages; foreign exchange risk, since 60% or more of sales revenue comes from abroad; increasing input prices (sugar and aluminum); competition from PepsiCo; and slow growth, which makes Coca-Cola vulnerable to changes in interest rates. It is definitely a defensive stock – a strong international brand, constant cash flow, and a consistent history of increasing dividends make it low-risk and reliable (unlike high-risk high-growth technology stocks).

## VOICE RULES (apply to every sentence)

Write in upper-formal undergraduate academic English. Clear, direct, confident. No contractions. No em-dashes. No semicolons. No colons. No rhetorical questions. No bullet points or numbered lists inside the body text. Punctuation: commas and periods only.

**Sentence length:** Mean 20 words. Nothing above 32 words except one statistics chain per section. Remove very short sentences under 10 words unless they open a paragraph as a topic sentence.

**Connectors — use only these, in this order of preference:**
- "This [noun] led to…" / "This decision…" / "This trend…" (most common)
- "Similarly, …" (parallel case)
- "Furthermore, … also …" (additive, note the doubled marker)
- "Despite this, …" (reversal after a statistic)
- "Although X…, it also Y" (intra-sentence concession)
- Never use: Moreover, However, Additionally, Therefore, Consequently, In conclusion

**Noun doublets:** Pair abstractions. Examples: "misunderstandings and conflicts", "prospects and difficulties", "innovation and creativity", "stereotypes and prejudices". Use 1–2 doublets per section.

**Time framing:** Open sentences about historical facts or statistics with the time frame first, then comma. Example: "In October 2025, gold prices exceeded 4,000 dollars per ounce for the first time."

**Citations:** No parenthetical (Author, Year) citations. State facts bare with specific names and dates. For numbers: "Statistics in [year] indicated that…"

**First person:** Only once, in the portfolio rationale section only, as this three-sentence chain:
1. "As someone who [everyday habit related to technology/money], I have noticed that…"
2. "This realization led me to reflect on…"
3. "Through research for this report, I discovered that…"
Then drop "I" for the rest of the document.

**Statistics chain rule:** When presenting 3 or more numbers, collapse into one descending comma-chain with the largest figure first, then follow with "Despite this, …" or "Even so, …"

**Imperfections to seed (final pass — one per 150–200 words, choose from list, not more than one of each):**
- One doubled discourse marker: "Furthermore, there are also…"
- One adjective used where an adverb belongs: "with especial attention to…"
- One vague colloquial summarizer: "Everything together, starting from [X], has led to…"
- One verbless fragment: "By Using [topic] as the primary reference in analyzing…"

**AI patterns to eliminate before finishing:**
- "It is worth noting"
- "delve," "landscape," "tapestry," "navigate," "illuminate," "pivotal"
- "seamlessly," "comprehensive," "multifaceted," "undeniably"
- "it becomes evident that"
- "This serves as a powerful illustration"
- Any sentence starting with "In conclusion"
- Three-item parallel lists (A, B, and C)
- Perfectly uniform sentence lengths

## AI-CHECK PASS (full detection — run after every section)

After writing each section, scan every sentence against ALL categories below. If anything matches, rewrite that sentence. Do not move to the next section until every check passes. Mark each section "[PASSED AI-CHECK]" before continuing.

### CHECK 1 — Banned vocabulary (rewrite any sentence containing these)
delve, tapestry, nuanced, multifaceted, underscore, intricate interplay, played a crucial role, complex and multifaceted, paramount, pivotal, meticulous, holistic, robust, stands as, serves as, marking a pivotal moment, underscores its importance, it is worth noting, in today's fast-paced world, at its core, seamlessly, comprehensive, undeniably, illuminate, navigate, landscape

### CHECK 2 — Banned structures
- Tricolon lists: "research, collaboration, and problem-solving" → break the three-item list, name one concretely and gesture at the rest
- Uniform sentence lengths: count words in 5 consecutive sentences — if they are all between 12 and 18 words, vary them
- Perfect paragraph uniformity: all paragraphs the same approximate length is a flag — vary paragraph lengths
- Negative parallelism: "It is not about X, it is about Y" → rewrite as a direct claim
- Template conclusions: any sentence beginning with "In summary," "In conclusion," "Overall," "To conclude,"
- Em-dashes used for emphasis: "It is not X — it is Y" → rewrite the whole sentence
- Elegant variation: cycling through synonyms (industry → sector → field → domain) → pick one word and keep it

### CHECK 3 — Content flags
- Importance puffery: "this is an important development," "this plays a crucial role," "this has significant implications" → state the specific consequence instead
- "-ing" attribution phrases: "making it a leading provider of…" / "positioning the company as…" / "establishing itself as…" → rewrite as a direct statement
- Promotional language: "boasts," "nestled in," "state-of-the-art," "cutting-edge," "world-class" → replace with plain factual description
- "Challenges and opportunities" as a section frame → never use this paired phrase

### CHECK 4 — Formatting flags
- Every key term bolded → bold maximum 2 terms per section, only for genuine technical labels
- All section headings in Title Case → use Sentence case for sub-headings
- Inline-header lists: "**Bold label:** description" pattern appearing more than twice → convert to prose

### CHECK 5 — Structural burstiness test
Read the last 5 sentences aloud. If they all end at roughly the same point and have the same rhythm, one sentence needs to be broken in half and another needs to be extended. Academic prose has rhythm variation.

### PASS CRITERIA
A section passes when: no banned vocabulary survives, no three-item parallel lists survive, sentence lengths vary noticeably, no template opener or closer, no puffery, and no formatting over-use. Write "[PASSED AI-CHECK]" and continue.

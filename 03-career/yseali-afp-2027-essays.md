## Essay 7.1 — Interest and Experience in Top Two Themes

*Prompt: Tell us about your interest and experience in your top two ranked themes. For each theme, what do you hope to learn about and experience during the Fellowship? What about your background and interests makes you competitive for these themes? (400-word limit)*

Economic Prosperity and Liberty and Freedom are the two themes that connect most closely to my experience as a Computer Engineering student and to work I have already done in Thailand.

In the summer of 2026, I worked as an IT intern at Agoda, which is one of Southeast Asia's largest online travel platforms and a company that connects millions of travelers across the region each month. My role focused on IT operations: provisioning laptops for new employees, maintaining internal devices across departments, and writing automation scripts to monitor the uptime of critical display infrastructure used by operations teams. This experience showed me that the economic activity a technology company generates depends entirely on systems that most people never interact with directly. The travel and tourism sector across ASEAN contributes hundreds of billions of dollars to regional economies each year, and companies like Agoda are part of that foundation. This realization made me more interested in understanding how digital infrastructure shapes economic outcomes at scale, not just within a single company but across an entire region. Furthermore, I also built and deployed a full-stack clinic management system as a university project, which digitized appointment scheduling and patient records for a small medical practice, and this smaller project showed me that the same principles of reliability and efficiency that matter at Agoda also matter in community-level services that directly affect people's economic lives.

For the Economic Prosperity theme, I hope to learn how American institutions support entrepreneurship and how technology moves from a student project into a product that serves a real market. In Thailand, engineering students develop strong technical skills but face fewer clear pathways from a working prototype to a sustainable company, and I want to understand what policies and networks make that difference in the United States.

As someone who operates a self-hosted home server with custom DNS configuration, firewall rules, and encrypted reverse proxy routing, I have noticed that controlling your own digital infrastructure is technically straightforward but almost completely unknown to ordinary people in Thailand. This realization led me to become more interested in the relationship between digital access and personal freedom. In several countries across ASEAN, access to information online is sometimes restricted, and most users do not know what choices or control they have. For the Liberty and Freedom theme, I hope to learn from American civil society organizations and legal experts how digital rights advocacy functions in practice, because my technical background gives me an understanding of these issues from the infrastructure level, and I want to connect that knowledge to effective community work.

## Essay 7.2 — Post-Fellowship Plan and US-ASEAN Shared Prosperity

*Prompt: United States Secretary of State Marco Rubio has stated: "The shared interests between the United States and the nations of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations are deeper today than at any point in our nations' histories." Describe your plan for applying the YSEALI AFP experience after you return home. How will participating in the Fellowship contribute to your future leadership and the United States' and ASEAN's shared prosperity? (400-word limit)*

The plan I am bringing back to Thailand after the Fellowship has one clear focus: closing the gap between the technical knowledge that exists in university engineering programs and the practical digital skills that ordinary Thai people, particularly those outside Bangkok, actually have access to.

Thailand has a growing technology sector centered in the capital, but digital literacy and awareness of data privacy remain low across much of the country, particularly in rural and semi-urban communities. Many people in these areas use smartphones every day but have no understanding of what data is being collected from them, how to recognize unsafe networks, or how to protect themselves and their families online. This is a problem that requires both technical knowledge and the ability to communicate that knowledge to people with no engineering background, which is exactly the combination I am building through my degree and through personal projects.

During the YSEALI Fellowship, I intend to learn from American organizations that have successfully translated technical expertise into public education, including groups working on digital rights, community technology access, and civic engagement. The United States has a long history of civil society organizations that operate independently and build public knowledge on issues that governments and corporations do not always prioritize. Returning to Thailand with direct exposure to how these organizations work, how they sustain themselves, and how they build trust with the communities they serve, would help me design something similar that fits the Thai context.

After completing my degree at KMITL, I plan to develop and run workshops on digital self-reliance, beginning in Bangkok and then expanding to community colleges, vocational schools, and local organizations in provinces where these topics have not yet reached. The practical skills I want to teach include recognizing phishing attempts and unsafe networks, understanding what smartphones collect, and, for participants with more interest, setting up basic privacy tools. Everything together, starting from the home server infrastructure I already operate and maintain myself, has given me the hands-on experience to teach these topics with real examples rather than theory alone.

The shared prosperity that the United States and ASEAN can build together depends in part on populations that are informed, digitally capable, and able to participate in a digital economy without being exploited by it. A Thai engineer who learns from American civil society and returns home to share those lessons with Thai communities is one concrete contribution to that shared goal, and this is the leadership role I am preparing for.
